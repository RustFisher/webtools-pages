Plot Voltage Curves from Pasted Serial Logs

Paste plain-text serial output and the page extracts the log time plus sample and system voltage values, then draws a line chart as you type. Parsing and drawing happen locally in your browser.

How to Plot a Serial Log

  1. Paste the serial log text into the input area, or load the sample.
  2. The page detects each current time line and attaches the sample voltage and system voltage lines that follow it.
  3. Drag across the chart to zoom, double-click to reset, and click a legend entry to hide a curve.
  4. Choose the X axis (log time, relative seconds, sys clock or index), then export a PNG or CSV when needed.

Supported Log Format

Each record starts at a current time line. Matching is case-insensitive and tolerates separators such as spaces, hyphens, underscores, ":" and "=". Timestamps, ANSI color codes and tool prefixes before the keywords are ignored.

sys clock:19481 status:2 esp_power:0 esp_status:0
current time: 2026/08/28 11:17:59
sample voltage: 3126 mV
system voltage: 3075 mV

Incomplete Records and Units

A record needs a time and at least one voltage value. A trailing time line without voltages is counted as incomplete and not plotted. Values in V are converted to mV.

Log times carry no time zone, so they are shown as written. If the time moves backwards, for example after a device restart, those points are skipped and reported.

Privacy and Limits

The log text never leaves your browser, although the website may load analytics scripts. The input is kept in local storage so a refresh does not lose it. Very large logs may feel slower while typing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my curve empty?

No record contained both a current time line and a voltage line. Check the keyword spelling and that the lines are in the same order as the example.

Can I plot other fields such as current?

Not in this version, which draws sample voltage and system voltage. The parser is rule-based so more fields can be added later.

Can it read the serial port directly?

Not yet. Copy the output from a terminal such as the Web Serial Terminal and paste it here.
